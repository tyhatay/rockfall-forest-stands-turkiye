# Can the specificity test pass?

200 draws in each of three worlds; only the outcome is simulated. The reporting world is calibrated to induce the same dose coefficient (gamma -3.0).

| World | Dose | Placebo | Joint dose | Joint placebo | Rule says failed | Reaches the observed placebo | Both |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| protection | -1.195 | -0.444 | -1.185 | -0.037 | 25% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| reporting | -1.094 | -1.993 | -0.590 | -1.788 | 100% | 37.5% | 23.5% |
| neither | +0.013 | -0.034 | +0.025 | -0.039 | 4% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| **observed** | **−1.197** | **−2.112** | **−0.669** | **−1.885** | failed | — | — |

The observed pattern lies inside what the reporting world produces and outside what the protective world produces. Applied as a binary rule the test also misfires on a quarter of protective draws, so what separates the worlds is the size of the placebo coefficient rather than whether its interval excludes zero.
